Finding a Voice People Can Recognise

A brand’s public voice should sound like the same organisation, wherever the conversation happens.

Define a point of view

A recognisable voice begins with a clear belief. What does the brand understand about its audience? What is it willing to say, and what can it support? Answer these questions in ordinary language before building a message framework.

Give the team a shared reference

Write down the central message, the reasons to believe it and a few practical examples. Include the words the brand uses naturally and the phrases it avoids. Make the guidance short enough to use during real work.

Keep the person in the message

Consistency does not require every spokesperson to sound identical. Give people room to speak in their own voice while keeping the underlying facts and commitments aligned. The result should feel clear, credible and human.
